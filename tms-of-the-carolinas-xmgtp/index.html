Frequently Asked Questions (Raleigh Location)

Q1: What is TMS therapy?

Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) is a non-invasive procedure that uses magnetic fields to stimulate nerve cells in the brain to improve symptoms of depression and other mental health conditions. It is typically used for individuals who have not responded adequately to previous treatments.

Q2: How does TMS work?

TMS works by using a device that generates magnetic pulses through a coil placed against the scalp. These pulses pass through the skull and target specific areas of the brain involved in mood control, helping to regulate brain activity associated with depression.

Q3: Is TMS therapy effective?

Research and clinical experience indicate that TMS can be highly effective for many individuals with treatment-resistant depression. Success rates vary, but TMS offers a promising alternative or addition to existing treatments.

Q4: Are there side effects of TMS?

TMS is generally well-tolerated, with common side effects including mild headache or scalp discomfort during treatment. These effects are usually temporary and subside after the session ends. Serious side effects are rare.

Q5: How long does TMS treatment take?

A typical TMS session lasts about 25-45 minutes. Treatment usually involves daily sessions, five days a week, for several weeks. The full course of treatment and duration can vary based on individual needs and response to therapy.
