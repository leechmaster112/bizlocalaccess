Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: What types of employment law issues do you handle for employees in Toronto?

A1: We handle a wide range of employment law issues for employees in Toronto, including wrongful dismissal, termination without cause or notice, severance pay disputes, workplace harassment and discrimination claims, failure to accommodate disabilities, constructive dismissal, and issues related to employment standards (wages, hours, vacation pay, termination pay).

Q2: Can you help me if I'm being asked to sign a release agreement before leaving my job?

A2: Yes, we can advise you on release agreements (often called compromise agreements or severance agreements). It is crucial to understand the terms of any agreement before signing, as they can significantly impact your rights and entitlements. We can review the agreement and negotiate terms that are fair and protect your interests.

Q3: How long do I have to file a claim after being terminated in Toronto?

A3: The time limits for filing employment-related claims in Ontario vary depending on the specific issue. For termination without cause or insufficient notice/severance, the limitation period is generally two years from the date you became aware of the dismissal. For human rights claims (harassment, discrimination), the limitation period is generally three years. It is essential to consult with us as soon as possible after an employment issue arises to ensure you meet all relevant deadlines.

Q4: Do you represent employers in employment disputes in Toronto?

A4: Yes, we also provide legal services to employers in the Toronto area. We help businesses understand and comply with employment legislation, draft employment contracts and policies, manage workplace investigations, and represent them in disputes with employees, including potential lawsuits or arbitrations.

Q5: What should I bring to my first consultation at your office in Toronto?

A5: For your first consultation at our Toronto office (45 St Clair Ave W #908), please bring any relevant documents related to your employment situation. This may include your employment contract, offer letter, performance reviews, HR communications regarding termination or the issue in question, any written policies from your employer, and any correspondence or emails related to the matter. Having these documents helps us understand your situation better and provide more effective advice.
