Frequently Asked Questions About TMS Therapy in Raleigh

What is TMS therapy and how does it work for depression in Raleigh?

TMS therapy, or Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation, is a non-invasive procedure that uses magnetic pulses to stimulate nerve cells in the brain. In Raleigh, it's primarily used to treat Major Depressive Disorder, particularly when patients haven't responded well to antidepressant medications. The magnetic pulses are thought to affect mood by influencing neurotransmitter activity and brain connectivity in areas associated with depression.

Are there side effects associated with TMS therapy in Raleigh?

TMS therapy is generally well-tolerated, and serious side effects are rare. The most common side effects are mild and temporary, such as headache, scalp discomfort at the treatment site, or fatigue. Seizures are a very rare but potential risk, which is why treatment is conducted under strict medical supervision.

Good candidates for TMS therapy in Raleigh typically include adults diagnosed with Major Depressive Disorder, especially those who have not achieved adequate relief from one or more antidepressant medications. What should I expect during a TMS therapy session in Raleigh?

During a TMS therapy session in Raleigh, you will sit comfortably in a chair while a device delivers magnetic pulses to specific areas of your scalp. The procedure is non-invasive, meaning no anesthesia is required. You will be awake and can resume normal activities immediately after the session. Sessions typically last between 20 to 40 minutes.
