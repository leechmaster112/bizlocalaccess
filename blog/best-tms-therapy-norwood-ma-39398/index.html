FAQ: Best TMS Therapy Norwood MA (2026)

What is TMS therapy, and how does it work?

TMS therapy, or Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation, is a non-invasive procedure that uses magnetic pulses to stimulate nerve cells in the brain. These pulses target specific areas of the brain involved in mood regulation, effectively treating depression and other mental health conditions. It's an outpatient procedure, meaning it does not typically require hospitalization.

What conditions can TMS therapy treat?

TMS therapy is most commonly used to treat major depressive disorder. However, it can also be effective in treating other conditions like anxiety disorders, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), PTSD and chronic pain. Research continues to explore the potential of TMS for other neurological and psychiatric illnesses.

Is TMS therapy covered by insurance?

Most insurance providers, including many commercial insurance companies and Medicare, cover TMS therapy for the treatment of depression that has not responded to other treatments. The specifics of coverage vary depending on your insurance plan, so it's essential to confirm your benefits with your provider before starting treatment. Onward Psychiatry can help you navigate this process.

How long does a TMS therapy session last?

A typical TMS therapy session lasts between 20 to 60 minutes. The length of the session can vary based on the specific treatment protocol prescribed by your psychiatrist. Generally, treatments are administered five days a week, over several weeks, which generally includes a "ramp up" and a "taper down" phase.

Are there any side effects of TMS therapy?

TMS therapy is generally considered a safe procedure. The most common side effects are mild and temporary, including headaches, scalp discomfort, or tingling at the site of stimulation. Serious side effects are rare but can include seizures. Your doctor will carefully screen you to minimize any risks.

How many sessions of TMS therapy are typically needed?

TMS treatment protocols typically involve a course of daily sessions, five times a week, for four to six weeks. The exact number of sessions and the duration of treatment may vary depending on your individual response to the treatment and the recommendation of your psychiatrist. Your psychiatrist at Onward Psychiatry will work closely with you throughout this time.

What should I expect during a TMS therapy session?

During a TMS session, you'll sit in a comfortable chair while a trained technician positions the TMS coil near your head. The coil delivers short magnetic pulses. You'll likely hear clicking sounds and feel a tapping sensation on your scalp. You will remain awake and alert throughout the procedure, and will be able to resume normal activities immediately after each session. You can ask your assigned professionals with Onward Psychiatry for details about your specific protocol.

How effective is TMS therapy?

TMS therapy has proven to be an effective treatment for major depressive disorder in many patients. Studies show remission rates and significant symptom reduction in a large percentage of individuals. The effectiveness can vary, and your doctor at Onward Psychiatry can provide realistic expectations based on current research and your personal medical history.

What if TMS therapy doesn’t work for me right away?

If TMS therapy does not show initial results, your psychiatrist may adjust the treatment parameters such as the frequency or intensity of stimulation. Some patients experience improvements after the initial treatment course. Your provider can also explore augmenting TMS with other therapies, such as medication adjustments or psychotherapy (like at Onward Psychiatry.)

Can I drive myself home after a TMS therapy session?

Yes, TMS therapy does not require any medications that would impact your ability to drive. You can drive yourself home immediately after a TMS session and continue with your normal daily activities.

How can I prepare for my first TMS therapy session?

Before your first TMS session, your provider will review your medical history, discuss the procedure in detail, and answer any questions you may have. It is recommended to eat a light meal before your session, and you can take any prescribed medications as usual. Be sure to arrive well-rested and prepared to relax.
